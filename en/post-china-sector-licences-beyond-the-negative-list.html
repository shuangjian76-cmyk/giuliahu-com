«Not on the prohibited list» is true, and it is not enough

Published on 11 October 2026 · Category: accesso al mercato

There are three gates into the Chinese market, not one. The negative list says whether a foreign investor may enter a sector; it does not say whether the activity needs a licence, and licences do not look at nationality.

It is a sentence one hears often, and it is correct: «my activity is not on the list of prohibited sectors». The point is that the list is one of three gates, and whoever checks only one tends to discover the other two after signing.

The three gates, in order

  • The negative list for foreign investment access: it says whether a foreign investor may enter that sector, and whether they may do so alone or must have a local partner.
  • The business scope recorded on the business licence: in China this is binding, not descriptive. An activity not recorded may not be carried on, and recording a regulated activity requires the relevant qualification.
  • The sector licence: it does not look at the investor's nationality. It applies to everyone, domestic companies included, and it is the gate that gets forgotten.

Why the third one is the surprise

The 2024 negative list removed all restrictions in manufacturing: in many processing activities a Chinese partner is no longer a legal requirement. That is good news, but it concerns only the first gate. Several service sectors — education and training, healthcare, food, transport — require a specific administrative licence, with requirements on premises, qualified staff, safety and sometimes minimum capital, granted at provincial level and therefore differing from province to province.

Where a sector licence exists, that licence is often the company's most valuable asset: it is tied to the premises, sometimes to a resident officer, and it does not move. If it is held by an entity of the partner, the foreign shareholder does not own a business — they own a stake in a business that lives on someone else's permit.

The two questions to ask before choosing the structure

The first: does the activity, as it will be recorded in the business licence, require a sector authorisation? The second decides the answer more often than one would expect: who the service is aimed at. The same content aimed at adults and aimed at minors falls, in several sectors, under two entirely different regimes.

Lists and requirements change and are published at national and provincial level: they must be checked as at the date of the transaction and for the province of establishment. This text is for information only.
