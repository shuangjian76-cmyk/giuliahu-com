The hard part isn't finding an advisor. It's no longer having to coordinate five of them.

Companies operating between Italy and China usually end up with four or five professionals who don't talk to each other: the accountant who doesn't know what the employment consultant signed, the lawyer who sees the contract after the fact, the engineer who shows up when the machine is already at customs. Every handover loses information, and the real cost isn't the fee — it's time.

I work the other way round. The file is gathered once, I hold the thread with every specialist, and you deal with one person, in your own language. Work reserved by law stays with the professionals on the relevant registers; the analysis, the coordination and the responsibility for the overall result stay with me.

And when the matter is entirely new?

No file arrives already familiar: the rules change, the sectors change, the countries change. What doesn't change is how fast a new file becomes mine. Learning an unfamiliar field thoroughly and quickly is the part of this work I do best — and where I don't yet know something, I say so beforehand, not afterwards.

Fiscomania Medium

Riforma TARI 2027: termine unico di 90 giorni e taglio alle sanzioni

What it means for you: Legislative Decree 147 of 7 August 2026, in force since 12 August 2026, rewrites the penalties on municipal taxes: from 1 January 2027 the TARI return has a single national deadline of 90 days from the event — new occupancy, change of surface or use, termination — replacing the deadlines set by each municipality. Penalties become fixed: 100% of the tax for a missing return, with a minimum of 50 euro, and 40% of the additional tax for an inaccurate one, replacing the former ranges of 100% to 200% and 50% to 100%. Before issuing an assessment the municipality must send a compliance letter giving 60 days to correct, and in that phase penalties do not apply in full. The point that matters on a notice already received: what counts is the date the violation was committed, so years before 2027 still carry the old penalties. If you open or close premises in 2027, count 90 days from the event, not the municipal deadline.

Società estera con cantiere in Italia: caso pratico tra IVA e stabile organizzazione

What it means for you: A foreign company can carry out a contract in Italy without setting up an Italian company, but two questions must be assessed separately because they follow different rules: VAT, which does not depend on the duration of the site, and permanent establishment, which depends precisely on it. In the case examined, a nine-month contract extended to fourteen changes an essential premise of the initial tax assessment, and the consequences can reach the period before the extension too: duration must be reassessed when the contract lengthens, not only when it is signed. On the VAT side, if the transaction is a supply of goods dispatched from abroad and installed in Italy by the supplier, the tax is territorially Italian (art. 15(1), Legislative Decree 10/2026) and, where no permanent establishment intervenes, it is accounted for by the Italian customer under the reverse charge (art. 64(2)); if instead the transaction qualifies as a service connected with immovable property, art. 18(1)(a) applies. Holding an Italian VAT number does not, by itself, change how you invoice. For a Chinese client the structure of the case holds, the applicable treaty does not: you read the Italy-China double taxation treaty, not the German one, and the construction-site thresholds may differ.

Vendita con riserva di proprietà: la revoca della risoluzione e imposta di registro

What it means for you: A sale with retention of title is subject to proportional registration tax from the moment of signing, while under civil law ownership passes only on payment of the final instalment (art. 1523 of the Civil Code); the treatment of acts subject to a suspensive condition is set out in art. 27 of Presidential Decree 131/1986. The first-instance Tax Court of Rome, in judgment no. 6811 of May 2026, excluded proportional tax on the act revoking an earlier declaration of termination for breach, finding it declaratory in nature: same parties, same subject matter, no new transfer of wealth, so taxing it would duplicate the levy. What really decides the outcome is not the name given to the act but a fact to be checked first: whether the declaration of breach had actually dissolved the contract — and on its own it does not, if a proper termination mechanism is missing. If the contract was already dissolved, restoring it may count as a new transfer of assets and become taxable again. This is a first-instance ruling: it works as a defensive argument, not as settled law.
